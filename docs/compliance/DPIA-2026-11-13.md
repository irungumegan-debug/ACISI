## 1. Why a DPIA is needed

ACISI processes **health data** (diagnoses, prescriptions, lab results, scans and reports), which is sensitive personal data under the Data Protection Act, on a large scale across several clinics, and includes children's data. Processing of this kind is likely to result in a high risk to data subjects, so a DPIA is required before and during processing.

## 2. Description of the processing

### 2.1 Nature and purpose
Clinics use ACISI to check patients in (front desk, patient website), manage the queue, record consultations, request lab tests and record results, store patient files, bill and take payment, and send receipts and reminders by SMS. Patients use the portal to check in remotely, see their own records, manage record sharing and upload their own files.

### 2.2 Roles
- **The clinic** is the data controller for visit and health records. **ACISI is the data processor** for those records and acts only on the clinic's written instructions (Data Processing Agreement signed before real patient data is entered).
- **ACISI is the data controller** for patient and staff accounts, the ACISI check-in fee, SMS sent on its own behalf, and website visitors.

### 2.3 Data subjects
Patients (including children, through a parent or guardian), next of kin, clinic staff (receptionists, clinicians, doctors, lab technicians, clinic admins), and website visitors.

### 2.4 Personal data processed

| Category | Data | Sensitive? |
|---|---|---|
| Patient identity | Name, phone (verified), ACISI ID, date of birth, sex, email (optional), ID document type and number (national ID, passport, birth certificate, alien ID; optional) | Sex and ID: yes |
| Next of kin | Name and phone (optional) | Yes (family details) |
| Visits | Clinic, department, reason for visit, check-in time, queue position, assigned doctor | Health: yes |
| Clinical | Diagnosis, prescription, lab tests requested, lab results (value, unit, flag, comment), uploaded files (X-rays, scans, lab reports, old paper records) | Yes |
| Consent and choices | Registration consent, record-sharing consent and its history, SMS opt-out, Privacy Notice and Terms acceptances | No |
| Payments | Bill lines, amounts, method, M-Pesa or card reference | No |
| Staff | Name, role, phone, work email (optional), staff ID, PIN (bcrypt hash only), actions in the audit log | No |
| Technical | Session cookies, audit records (IDs only, no names or clinical text), access logs without query strings | No |

ACISI never stores card numbers or M-Pesa PINs.

### 2.5 Lawful basis
- Health care and visit records: provision of health care by the clinic (necessary for health services, Data Protection Act s.30 and s.45 for health data).
- Payments: performance of contract; financial records: legal obligation.
- ID number and date of birth: identifying the patient correctly and avoiding duplicate records (legitimate interest of the clinic in safe care).
- Record sharing between clinics, and walk-in invitation SMS: **explicit consent**, recorded with version, date and source; withdrawable at any time.
- Staff accounts and audit logs: legitimate interest in a secure system.

### 2.6 Recipients and where data goes

| Recipient | What | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Clinic staff | Only what their role needs (see 4.2) | — |
| Other clinics | Visit history and files only with the patient's standing consent or a one-time code the patient gives that day | — |
| HostAfrica (hosting) | All data, on ACISI's own server | Nairobi, Kenya |
| Africa's Talking (SMS) | Patient phone number and SMS text (no diagnosis, no test names or results) | Kenyan company; written confirmation of where content is processed is still awaited |
| Safaricom (M-Pesa) | Phone number, amount, reference | Kenya |
| Cloudflare (DNS only, proxy off) | Directs visitors to the server; never sees content | — |
| Google (email) | Only messages people send to the support email | May be outside Kenya; patients are told not to send health details by email |

No patient records are transferred outside Kenya. Courts, the ODPC or the Digital Health Agency receive data only on a lawful request.

### 2.7 Retention
Visit and health records: while a patient of the clinic and 7 years after the last visit. Payment and tax records: 7 years. SMS, website and security logs: 12 months. Staff accounts: while active and 12 months after. Pending signups: deleted after 14 days. Backups: 14 days (uploaded-file archives: newest 3 copies). Account deletion wipes the patient's identifying details and their own uploaded files; clinic records stay, anonymised.

## 3. Necessity and proportionality

- **Minimum data:** only name and phone are required. Everything else (ID, date of birth, sex, next of kin, email) is optional.
- **Data quality:** phone verified by SMS code or in person; ID numbers checked for format; one record per phone number.
- **Transparency:** Privacy Notice shown at signup, at each clinic's first check-in and walk-in; acceptance recorded with its version.
- **Data subject rights:** access (patients see and download all their visits), correction (front desk and patient portal), deletion (patient can delete their account), portability (full PDF download), objection and consent withdrawal (sharing and SMS can be turned off at any time). Response times follow ODPC guidance and are published in the Privacy Notice.
- **Processors:** clinics sign a Data Processing Agreement; staff accept the Confidentiality Undertaking before using the console.

## 4. Security measures in place

### 4.1 Technical
- HTTPS only (TLS 1.2/1.3, HSTS); strict browser security headers; no other sites can embed ACISI.
- Server in Kenya; SSH by key only, no root login, firewall (ports 22, 80, 443 only), fail2ban, automatic security updates.
- Health data (diagnoses, prescriptions, visit reasons, lab notes and results), ID numbers and next-of-kin details encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM; uploaded files encrypted on disk; key kept in the root-only settings file and offline.
- PINs and passwords stored only as bcrypt hashes; accounts lock for 5 minutes after 5 wrong PINs; automatic logoff after 15 minutes without activity; sessions also expire (staff 8 h, clinic admin 6 h, owner 2 h).
- Audit log append-only and hash-chained in the database; any alteration is detectable.
- Doctors sign consultations and lab requests, and lab technicians sign results, by re-entering their PIN; signed records cannot be edited.
- Nightly encrypted backups (age, private key kept offline), 14 days, monthly restore test.
- Uploaded files: type checked by content, 20 MB limit, private folder, random names, never public links; uploads refused when the disk is low.
- Logs contain no login tokens, cookies, search text or personal data; audit records hold IDs only.
- Background jobs keep only recent finished jobs; disk and log size are capped (after the 13 November 2026 outage).

### 4.2 Access control (least privilege)
- **Front desk / admin:** patients seen at their clinic; never diagnoses or lab results on screen (only on a printed record the patient asks for, which is logged).
- **Doctors:** only patients in their own department queue.
- **Lab technicians:** only open lab requests: patient name, ACISI ID, age, sex, tests and the doctor's note. Never phone numbers, diagnoses, prescriptions, history or billing.
- **Other clinics:** only with the patient's consent or same-day code; every such view is logged.
- **ACISI owner site:** counts and clinic settings, no clinical records.

### 4.3 Organisational
Named Data Protection Officer; Confidentiality Undertaking for everyone with access; clinic Data Processing Agreements; staff deactivated when they leave; breach procedure (clinic told within 48 hours, ODPC within 72 hours, affected patients when at real risk); written runbook for operations and recovery; annual review of security and the Privacy Notice.

## 5. Risk assessment

Likelihood and severity: Low / Medium / High. Residual risk is after the measures listed.

| # | Risk to data subjects | Likelihood | Severity | Measures in place | Residual |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Staff member views records they don't need | Medium | High | Role-based access, queue scoping, logged views, Confidentiality Undertaking | Low |
| R2 | Login stolen or guessed | Medium | High | Personal logins, bcrypt, lockout, session expiry, PIN re-entry to sign | **Medium** (no multi-factor login yet, see A2) |
| R3 | Server disk, snapshot or provider access exposes stored data | Low | High | Server in Kenya, SSH key only, firewall; backups encrypted; health data, ID numbers and files encrypted at rest (AES-256-GCM) | Low (names and phone numbers stay unencrypted for search) |
| R4 | Data lost (hardware failure, mistake, ransomware) | Low | High | Nightly encrypted backups, restore tests, written recovery steps | **Medium** (backups are on the same server, see A3) |
| R5 | Service unavailable (patients can't check in) | Medium | Medium | Health check, runbook, disk and log caps, job clean-up | **Medium** (no uptime alert yet, see A4) |
| R6 | Health details seen by others on a shared phone | Medium | Medium | SMS never contain diagnosis, tests or results; SMS opt-out | Low |
| R7 | Records shared between clinics without consent | Low | High | Sharing off by default; consent or same-day code; logged | Low |
| R8 | Audit log altered to hide misuse | Low | Medium | Database refuses changes and deletions; hash chain makes any alteration detectable | Low |
| R9 | SMS provider processes content outside Kenya | Low | Medium | Minimal SMS content | **Medium** (written confirmation pending, see A6) |
| R10 | Inaccurate or duplicate patient records | Medium | Medium | Verified phone, ID format checks, one record per phone | Low |
| R11 | Children's data mishandled | Low | High | Guardian present at the clinic and consents; guardian's phone used; portal signup refuses under-18s | Low |
| R12 | Malicious file uploaded | Low | Medium | Content type checks, size limits, served without scripts | Low |
| R13 | Data kept longer than needed | Medium | Low | Retention periods set and published | **Medium** (automatic deletion at end of retention not yet built, see A7) |

## 7. Consultation
Clinic partners' feedback is gathered through onboarding and support. The ODPC will be consulted before processing if a residual risk is assessed as high and cannot be reduced. No residual risk is currently assessed as high.

## 8. Conclusion and sign-off
The processing is necessary and proportionate for providing health services. With the measures in place, residual risks are low or medium; the medium risks have actions with owners and dates in section 6. Processing may continue, with this DPIA reviewed when an action is completed, when processing changes, and at least once a year.
