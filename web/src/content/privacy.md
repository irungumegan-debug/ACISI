# ACISI Privacy Notice

This notice explains, in plain language, what personal data ACISI collects, why, who sees it and what your rights are under Kenya's Data Protection Act, 2019.

## Who are we and how can you contact us?

ACISI Healthcare is a business registered in Kenya (business name registration number BN-L3SMWLDM). We provide ACISI, a records and administration system that clinics use for patient check-in, queues, visit records, prescriptions, payments and receipts.

ACISI is not a healthcare provider. We do not diagnose, treat, or give medical advice. Your care comes from the clinic you visit. For any questions about your data, contact our Data Protection Officer, Megan Irungu (Founder), at acisi.help@gmail.com or 0746404155. For anything else, reach us at acisi.help@gmail.com or 0746404155.

## Who is this notice for, and who is responsible for your data?

This notice covers patients, clinic owners and staff (receptionists, doctors and administrators), and visitors to acisi.co.ke.

The clinic you visit is responsible for your visit and health records, as their data controller. ACISI is the data processor for those records: we hold and process them only on the clinic's written instructions.

ACISI is the data controller for your ACISI account, the check-in fee, the SMS we send on our own behalf, and information about visitors to our website. For clinic staff accounts and activity logs, ACISI is responsible for the account itself, while the clinic stays responsible for the patient records its staff works with.

Each clinic signs a Data Processing Agreement with ACISI before any real patient data is entered. For questions about your medical care, contact your clinic. For anything about how ACISI handles your data, contact our Data Protection Officer.

## What personal data do we collect?

We collect only what the clinic and ACISI need to check you in, keep your records and handle payments.

From patients, we collect your name, phone number, sex, date of birth and residence, and the name and phone number of your next of kin. To identify you correctly, we also record your national ID, passport, birth certificate or alien number. You give us these yourself, or clinic staff record them when you check in.

For each visit, we record the clinic, department, reason for visit, check-in time and your place in the queue. The clinic and the doctors who treat you add your health data: consultation notes, diagnosis, prescriptions, test results and appointments.

We also keep payment records: the check-in fee, your visit bill, how you paid, the amount and the M-Pesa or card reference. These come from you, the clinic and Safaricom. We record the choices you make too, such as opting out of SMS, agreeing to the check-in invitation, or consenting to share your records with another clinic.

From clinic staff, we collect name, role, work email, phone number, login details and the actions they take in ACISI. From clinics, we collect the clinic's name, location, departments, contact person and till or paybill number. From website visitors, we collect device and browser details, pages visited and any messages sent through our contact form.

Health data, your sex and family details such as next of kin are sensitive personal data under the Data Protection Act. We give them extra protection, and treat identity numbers with the same care. We never ask for or store card numbers or M-Pesa PINs.

## Why do we use it, and on what legal basis?

Every use of your data has a lawful basis under the Data Protection Act. We never sell your data or use health data for advertising.

We check you in, place you in the queue, keep your visit and health records, and send visit summaries, receipts and appointment reminders by SMS because these are needed to provide your health services. Collecting the check-in fee and recording payments is part of our contract with you and the clinic.

We use your ID number and date of birth to identify you correctly and avoid duplicate records, which the clinic needs to treat you safely and meet its legal obligations. The clinic may contact your next of kin when your care requires it, or in an emergency.

Some uses need your consent. Another clinic can see your history only with your explicit consent, given each time. We send walk-in patients an invitation to check in from home only if they agree.

We keep staff accounts, logins and activity logs, and use the least data needed to fix and improve ACISI, because we have a legitimate interest in running a secure, reliable system. We keep financial and tax records because the law requires it.

## What happens if you don't give us your data?

You can still be seen at the clinic, but some things may not work.

Your name and phone number are needed to check you in, keep your records together and reach you; without them, the clinic may need to register you on paper. Your ID number, date of birth, sex and residence help the clinic identify you correctly, so without them your records may be harder to match and the clinic may ask again. Next of kin details are optional, but without them the clinic cannot reach anyone on your behalf. SMS is optional too: if you opt out, ask the clinic for a printed or on-screen receipt and summary.

## What messages do we send you?

We send two kinds of SMS and treat them differently.

Service messages are part of your care: check-in confirmations, visit summaries, payment receipts and appointment reminders. Visit summaries list your medicines and how to take them, but leave out your diagnosis in case someone else reads your phone.

Invitations, such as asking walk-in patients to check in from home next time, are sent only with your consent and never contain health information.

We never use your health data for marketing. To stop SMS, tell the clinic staff or change the setting in your patient portal.

## How do payments work?

When you check in remotely, you pay ACISI's check-in fee by M-Pesa, and we receive the amount, your phone number and the M-Pesa reference. Walk-in patients don't pay this fee.

Payment for your visit goes straight to the clinic's own till, paybill or card machine, never to ACISI. ACISI records the amount, method and reference so the clinic can track payments and send your receipt.

Safaricom processes M-Pesa payments under its own privacy terms, and the clinic's card provider processes card payments.

## Who do we share your data with, and how is it protected?

Only people and organisations who need it, and only what they need. ACISI and everyone working for us are bound by a duty of confidentiality, as the law requires for anyone handling health data who is not a healthcare provider.

Your clinic's staff see what their role needs, and doctors see only patients in their own department queue. Every staff member has a personal login, and every view and change is logged. Another clinic can see your history only with your explicit consent, each time.

Our servers are hosted by HostAfrica in its data centre in Kenya. Our SMS provider, Africa's Talking, receives your phone number and the text of the messages we send, which carry as little health information as possible. Safaricom receives the payment amount, your phone number and the M-Pesa reference when you pay, and handles them under its own legal obligations.

We share data with courts, the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner or the Digital Health Agency only when the law requires it, on a lawful request.

We do not sell or rent personal data to anyone.

## Where is your data stored?

All patient data is stored and processed on servers in a data centre in Nairobi, Kenya. Kenyan law treats data used for primary or secondary health care as a matter of strategic national interest, so we keep it in Kenya.

Website traffic travels encrypted straight between your device and our server in Kenya. Our domain provider, Cloudflare, only directs visitors to the server and never sees the content. SMS messages are sent through Africa's Talking, a Kenyan company. We do not transfer patient records outside Kenya.

If you email us, or send a message through our contact form, it reaches our email provider, Google, which may store it outside Kenya. Please do not send health details by email; your clinic and ACISI keep those inside the system. If that ever changes, we will meet the Data Protection Act's conditions for cross-border transfers first, including your explicit consent for health data, and update this notice.

## How do we keep your data safe?

We use technical and organisational measures suited to health data.

Your data is encrypted while it travels between your device and ACISI. Our server is protected by a firewall, accepts administrator logins only with a security key, and installs security updates automatically. Every staff member has a personal login, and each role sees only what it needs. Patients log in to the portal with a PIN, and can reset it with a one-time code sent to their phone. Every view and change of records and payments is logged, and payments entered by mistake are voided with a reason, never deleted. Backups are made every night, encrypted, and kept in Kenya for 14 days.

Everyone with access to ACISI signs a confidentiality undertaking, and staff access is removed as soon as someone leaves a clinic. SMS carry as little health information as possible. We review our security, and this notice, at least once a year.

## How long do we keep your data, and how do we dispose of it?

We keep data only as long as there is a clear reason, never "just in case".

We keep visit and health records while you are a patient of the clinic, then for 7 years after your last visit. Payment and tax records are kept for 7 years. SMS logs, website and security logs are kept for 12 months. Staff accounts are kept while active and for 12 months after, and contact form messages are kept for 12 months after the conversation ends.

**Disposal.** When the period ends, we permanently delete electronic data, including backup copies as they roll over, or anonymise it so no one can be identified. Any printed material is shredded. When a clinic stops using ACISI, it can export its records first, then we delete or return them as our agreement with that clinic sets out.

## What are your rights, and how quickly do we respond?

Under the Data Protection Act you have the rights below, and we respond within the times the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC) sets.

You have the right to be told how your data is used, which this notice does. You can ask for a copy of the data we hold about you, and we respond within 7 days.

You can ask us to correct wrong or incomplete data, or to delete data where the law allows, and we respond to each within 14 days. Some health and payment records must be kept for a set period, so we cannot always delete them.

You can ask for your data in a format you can take elsewhere, and we respond within 30 days. You can object to some uses of your data, and we respond within 14 days. You can withdraw any consent you gave, such as for record sharing or invitations, at any time, and it takes effect immediately.

To use a right, email acisi.help@gmail.com or ask at your clinic. We may ask you to confirm your identity first. For records your clinic controls, we pass your request to the clinic and help it respond within these times.

## What happens if your data is lost, stolen or accessed without permission?

We act fast and tell the people who need to know.

We first contain the problem and investigate what happened. We tell the affected clinic within 48 hours of becoming aware of it, and the data controller, whether the clinic or ACISI for its own data, notifies the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner within 72 hours. If the breach puts you at real risk, you are told directly, with steps you can take to protect yourself. We record every breach and what we changed to stop it happening again.

## Children's data

A child's visit is checked in and recorded with a parent or guardian present, who gives consent on the child's behalf where the law requires it. The parent or guardian's phone number is used for the child's records and SMS. Children under 18 cannot create their own patient portal account.
